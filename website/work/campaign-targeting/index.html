Reach likely responders within a limited call budget

The decision

If a team can contact only a fifth of its audience, which records should it prioritize and how many historical responders would that capture?

My contribution: I framed the question, prepared and validated the data, performed the analysis, built the reporting output and wrote the recommendations.

How I approached it

Scoring-time contract

Used 45,211 historical contact records. Excluded call duration and current campaign count, plus current contact channel and timing, from the pre-contact candidate.

Model selection

Compared logistic regression and random forest with five-fold stratified cross-validation within the training data. Chose average precision / PR-AUC for the imbalanced response outcome.

Untouched test evaluation

Measured PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, lift and responder capture on the held-out test partition. Used permutation importance to interpret the ranking.

Campaign capacity

Compared 10%, 20%, 30% and 50% outreach bands. Recommended a controlled pilot whose thresholds reflect actual call costs, staffing and customer value.

Results and interpretation

0.738test ROC-AUC
0.384test PR-AUC
51.8%responders captured in top 20%
30.3%observed conversion in that band

The top 20% of ranked test records captured 51.8% of historical responders at 2.59× lift over the test baseline. This is a useful capacity scenario: a manager can see what is gained in response efficiency and lost in reach.

What I recommended

Run a randomized prospective pilot against existing targeting. Track incremental subscriptions, contact cost, opt-outs and complaints; review feature suitability before operational use.

What the evidence does not establish

The 2.59× lift is predictive performance on historical data, not causal campaign lift or achieved cost savings. The dataset lacks stable customer IDs and a full timestamp, so grouped and reliable out-of-time validation are unavailable. Scores rank records; they are not calibrated probabilities.
