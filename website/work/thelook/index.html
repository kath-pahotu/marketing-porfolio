The decision

Which parts of the customer journey deserve attention when acquisition, orders and customer behavior live in separate files?

My contribution: I framed the question, prepared and validated the data, performed the analysis, built the reporting output and wrote the recommendations.

How I approached it

Reproducible ELT

Loaded seven original files into DuckDB, normalized fields in staging, rebuilt sessions from raw events and created conformed dimensions, facts and decision marts.

Commercial KPI definitions

Separated gross demand from net sales by excluding cancelled and returned item value. Kept order, item, session and customer grains explicit.

Funnel and customer analysis

Analyzed session conversion, cart abandonment, cohort retention and RFM segments. Kept session traffic source separate from customer acquisition source because their taxonomies differ.

Validation and BI

Reconciled counts and KPI totals, enforced data contracts and modeled Power BI without fact-to-fact relationships. Excluded invalid shipment timestamps from delivery metrics.

Results and interpretation

124,814orders across Jan 2019-May 2024
680,862sessions rebuilt from events
58.0%cart abandonment in the sample
37.3%repeat-customer rate in the sample

Net sales reconciled to $8,096,531 across 80,095 purchasing customers. Session conversion was 26.6%. Similar cart-abandonment rates across channels suggest testing the onsite journey before assuming that media allocation is the main cause.

What I recommended

Prioritize an onsite checkout hypothesis and differentiated lifecycle treatments. Use net sales and repeat behavior to evaluate them; verify the result with a prospective test.

What the evidence does not establish

TheLook is synthetic. Its $8.10M net sales is a dataset result, not revenue I generated. May 2024 is incomplete. Marketing spend is absent, so CAC and ROAS cannot be calculated. A return-propensity model achieved ROC-AUC about 0.50 and was not recommended for deployment.
