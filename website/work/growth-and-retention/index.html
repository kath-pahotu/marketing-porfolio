The decision

How should a marketplace allocate subsidies and channel budgets when top-line growth, acquisition volume and durable customer value point in different directions?

My contribution: I framed the question, prepared and validated the data, performed the analysis, built the reporting output and wrote the recommendations.

How I approached it

Partner growth and subsidies

Separated absolute GMV from discount intensity, compared partner segments and funding mix, and developed a four-quadrant investment framework. Used weighted forecasts as directional scenarios.

Retention and user journeys

Decomposed first-month retention by acquisition source, region, service and customer segment. Distinguished app re-entry, early intent and checkout bottlenecks.

Acquisition and channel quality

Investigated divergence between installs and activated users. Compared channel spend, acquisition, bookings, GMV, CAC and cost-to-income ratios, with CPC measurement where definitions support it.

Data quality and measurement limits

Corrected national-versus-regional aggregation and false-positive decline alerts. Kept top-line spend ratios separate from profit-based ROAS and contribution measures.

What the analysis supports

Partner investmentscale and subsidy intensity considered together
Retention prioritiesre-entry and checkout diagnosed separately
Channel decisionsactivation and downstream value added to volume
Public evidencequalitative only; all source figures withheld

The work translated three business questions into partner investment priorities, retention hypotheses and controlled budget-change guardrails. Validation of totals and alert rules was part of the analysis, not a separate reporting afterthought.

What I recommended

Test focused subsidy, funnel and channel changes with acquisition, value and efficiency guardrails. Use holdouts or suitable controlled designs to estimate incrementality; collect cohort contribution data before making payback claims.

What the evidence does not establish

This is an independent case study, not employment at the company. Public material omits the company name, partner/channel identities and internal figures. Aggregate observational data supports diagnostic hypotheses, not causal uplift. Completed analysis used Excel. SQL/Python rebuilding, causal incrementality tests and cohort ROAS/payback are proposed next steps.
