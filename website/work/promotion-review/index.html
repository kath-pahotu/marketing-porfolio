Digital wallets and promotion economics: the business behind the work

Promotions spend money to encourage customer use. A review team needs to understand where credited cost concentrates without treating unusual behaviour as proven abuse.

How the work connects: Independent review prioritization connects budget exposure with operational capacity and customer-impact guardrails. It does not establish saved budget.

Mobility & deliveryBank marketingRokt holdout analysis

The challenge

Determine where a promotion team should focus review effort without treating unusual behavior as confirmed abuse.

How I delivered the work

  • Analyzed approximately 254,000 transactions with SQL, Python and Power BI.
  • Combined six behavioral signals, reconciled calculations and simulated review rules.
  • Designed an explainable report showing cost concentration and the trade-off between review workload and coverage.

What the work showed

Cost concentration

4.26% of scored users were associated with 26.94% of credited promotion cost.

Review design

Threshold simulation showed how changing the rule affects workload and exposed cost coverage.

Operational review queue

A separate balanced rule selects 1,994 users (2.31%) covering 23.89% of total credited campaign cost. This is a different cohort from the 4.26% score-based group; neither establishes confirmed abuse.

The marketing decision

Where this leads

Start with manual shadow-mode review, collect outcomes, and evaluate customer harm as well as potential budget recovery before automating any action.

Project evidence

Context and sources

Independent portfolio analysis, not employment at ZaloPay or a deployed policy. There are no confirmed fraud labels, measured savings or campaign-attributed retention results. Flags indicate review priority only.
